Requirement changes are a plague that has by now hit almost every AI/CS group of students doing an industry project.

As you can imagine, it’s ‘lovely’ to have to change the implementation of your 6-month project and your documentation two weeks before the end of the course/project because your client saw the beta-version and thinks you should have done things differently or wanted your project to focus on something else.

You may be inclined to think it’s your client's fault for “changing their mind” but the reality is that it’s your fault for not understanding the core problem that is causing the symptoms you are asked to treat with your project.

Here’s the nice part:

These requirement changes stop when you understand the core issue and treat it instead of the symptoms. That means: if you do the root cause analysis early on then you don’t have to grow white hairs at the end because you have to redo the whole thing. So, please do this early to avoid getting yourself hit by the plague of requirement changes at a later stage of your project.

What is root cause analysis?

Root cause analysis (RCA) is the process of discovering the root causes of problems in order to identify appropriate solutions. 

RCA assumes that it is much more effective to systematically prevent and solve the  underlying issues rather than just treating ad hoc symptoms and putting out fires.
